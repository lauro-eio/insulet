# Session 2 — Instructor Agenda & Facilitator Guide

**Status:** LOCKED (delivery copy)  
**Module:** Performance, Discipline, Separations & Workplace Safety  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Audience:** Managers outside Mexico overseeing direct or indirect Mexico-based reports  
**Objective:** Drive home that undocumented separations, sitting on misconduct, or ignoring workplace complaints creates massive legal exposure — pivot managers from independent decision-making to mandatory Employee Relations (ER) escalation.

**Still needed to run the room (not in this lock):** slide deck · Handout 2 (consequence matrix + escalation directory) · Fact vs Opinion visual · live poll paste text.

### Instructor guardrails

* **Tone:** Firm, urgent, protective — documentation and immediate ER involvement are the company's safeguards; instructor is compliance partner, not legal lecturer.
* **Time trap:** Do not teach complex severance formulas or NOM questionnaire details. Frame severance as high financial exposure (aggregate salary shape only) and NOM as mandatory HR protocols — keep manager focus on logging, reporting, and escalating.

## 2. Module 1: Performance, Discipline & Separations (25 mins)

### Part A: Performance Management & Progressive Discipline (10 mins)

* **What to Present (Slides):**
  * Progressive Discipline Framework: Setting expectations, documenting specific factual gaps, and ER-partnered warnings/PIPs.
  * High-Risk Red Warning Callout: **The 30-Day Misconduct Rule**.
  * Consistency Rule: Inconsistent disciplinary enforcement triggers discrimination claims.

* **What to Say (Script):**
  > "Poor performance alone rarely constitutes legal cause for separation under Mexican labor law. Never say 'it's not working out' or treat poor performance as an instant firing defense.
  >
  > Document facts, dates, and measurable impacts—not opinions or labels. Crucially, if an employee commits a specific act of misconduct, Mexican labor law gives us a strict 30-day window from the moment we become aware of it to issue formal notice. If you sit on misconduct, we forfeit the right to rely on it.
  >
  > Finally, treat similar situations consistently across your team. Applying different standards to different employees turns a simple performance issue into a high-risk discrimination claim."

* **What to Do (Facilitation):**
  * Run a 1-minute "Fact vs. Opinion" verbal exercise: Contrast "John has an uncooperative attitude" with "John missed 3 client deliverables on Sept 4, 10, and 12".

### Part B: Justified vs. Unjustified Separations & CFCRL Conciliation (15 mins)

* **What to Present (Slides):**
  * Separation Matrix: Justified (*Con Causa*) vs. Unjustified (*Sin Causa*) vs. Mutual Agreement.
  * Financial Risk Callout: Aggregate Severance Exposure (3 months salary + seniority pay + accrued benefits — shape of exposure, not a calculator).
  * Workflow Map: Mandatory Pre-Trial Conciliation (CFCRL).

* **What to Say (Script):**
  > "Ending employment without a legally proven cause is an unjustified dismissal, which triggers full statutory severance. Note that severance in Mexico is calculated on 'aggregate salary'—which includes base pay plus bonuses, allowances, and benefits—making real-world payouts significantly higher than base-salary estimates.
  >
  > Under Mexican labor law reforms, disputes pass through mandatory pre-trial conciliation at the CFCRL before any lawsuit can be filed. Negotiated exits ratified at the conciliation center give both sides legal certainty.
  >
  > Because conciliation is a legally binding process, **never communicate a termination, suggest a resignation, or discuss severance numbers alone**. Stop immediately and involve ER and Legal."

* **What to Do (Facilitation):**
  * Emphasize the absolute stop sign on the slide: "STOP before having any exit conversation with an employee".

## 3. Module 2: Safety, Anti-Discrimination & Compliance Matrix (25 mins)

* **What to Present (Slides):**
  * Mandatory Protocols Overview: Non-Discrimination, Workplace Violence, NOM-035 (Psychosocial Risk), and NOM-037 (Remote Work).
  * Manager Core Responsibilities: Recognize → Report Immediately → Protect Confidentiality → Zero Retaliation.
  * Consequence Matrix Highlight (Ch 8): Financial, legal, and reputational costs of non-compliance.

* **What to Say (Script):**
  > "Mexico requires strict protocols for preventing discrimination, workplace violence, and psychosocial risks under standards like NOM-035 and remote work guidelines under NOM-037.
  >
  > As a manager, if an employee brings a report of harassment, bullying, or discrimination to you, your duty is straightforward: **Recognize, report immediately to ER, maintain confidentiality, and enforce zero retaliation**.
  >
  > Never attempt to investigate a complaint yourself or keep it 'off the record'. Ignoring or mishandling a harassment complaint allows the employee to resign with cause attributable to the company, forcing us to pay full statutory severance plus facing regulatory sanctions."

## 4. Interactive Scenario Assessments (20 mins)

* **What to Say (before polls):**

### Scenario 3: The Undocumented Underperformer

* **Prompt:** A Mexico-based employee has missed performance targets for 6 months. The manager has held informal catch-ups but has no written records, PIP, or formal warnings on file. The manager wants to terminate the employee on Friday for "cause due to poor performance." What is the legal risk and correct next step?

* **Live Poll Options:**
  * A) Proceed with termination on Friday, explaining that 6 months of missed targets constitutes legal cause.
  * B) Offer 1 month of base salary as informal severance directly to the employee to settle quickly.
  * C) Halt any termination discussion. Contact Employee Relations to evaluate the case, build a proper performance record, or structure an HR/Legal-led exit.

* **Instructor Debrief & Key:**
  * **Correct Answer:** **C**.
  * **The Trap:** Option A fails because undocumented poor performance is not legal cause under the FLL, leading to an unjustified dismissal claim. Option B violates payroll rules and underestimates aggregate severance exposure.
  * **Escalation Path:** Contact Employee Relations immediately to review history and outline a compliant path forward.

### Scenario 4: The "Off-the-Record" Harassment Report

* **Prompt:** An employee informs their manager via video call that a senior team member has been sending inappropriate, hostile messages. The employee asks the manager to "keep it between us and not report it to HR because I fear retaliation." What must the manager do?

* **Live Poll Options:**
  * A) Respect the employee's request for privacy and try to mediate informally between the two team members.
  * B) Reassure the employee regarding anti-retaliation policies and immediately report the incident to Employee Relations/HRBP.
  * C) Wait to see if another incident occurs before escalating to HR.

* **Instructor Debrief & Key:**
  * **Correct Answer:** **B**.
  * **The Trap:** Options A and C create massive financial and legal exposure. Managers cannot hold harassment complaints "off the record" or conduct private investigations. Failing to report allows the employee to terminate employment with cause attributable to the employer (rescission), triggering full severance liabilities and authority sanctions.
  * **Escalation Path:** Report immediately to Employee Relations at `er@insulet.com`.
