4. Claims we make (simplified playbook language)

These are the teaching claims distilled for managers. Please flag any overstatement or incorrect routing.

Workshop materials are an internal manager reference. They do not replace Mexican labor law, employment agreements, company policy, or case-specific Legal/HR advice.

Golden rule

  • When in doubt, never act alone. Partner with HRBP / ER / Legal / Payroll before high-risk people actions.

Session 1 — Contracts & hours

  • No at-will employment in Mexico. Do not say or write “at-will,” “probationary at-will,” or “we can let you go / part ways anytime.”
  • Rights from Day 1, including trial / training periods.
  • Indefinite contracts are the default. Fixed-term only with real, verifiable justification; Legal approval.
  • Auto-conversion: If a fixed-term employee continues working past the end date (even one day), the relationship converts by law to indefinite.
  • Flag fixed-term expirations weeks in advance — do not invent a statutory “30-day contract” clock (that clock is for misconduct notice in Session 2).
  • Core-activity subcontracting / “contractors” for ongoing Insulet work: stop and route to HR/Legal (2021 reforms).
  • Overtime: voluntary, pre-authorized, logged in Workday (same day or week). Cap: 12 extraordinary hours/week (4 hrs/day, max 4 days). Exceeding the cap → Legal/Payroll before implementation.
  • No substitutions: no flat cash, gift cards, allowances, or informal comp time in place of overtime pay.
  • Managers do not calculate overtime premiums or statutory benefit amounts — Payroll does; managers authorize and record hours accurately.

Session 2 — Discipline, exits & safety

  • Poor performance alone rarely equals legal cause. Document facts/dates/impact — not labels. Partner with ER on warnings/PIPs.
  • 30-day misconduct clock: sitting on misconduct can forfeit reliance on that cause (prompt notice window from awareness).
  • Consistency: unequal discipline for similar conduct creates discrimination / pretext risk.
  • Never communicate a termination, suggest resignation, or discuss severance alone. Stop; ER/Legal lead; CFCRL conciliation for exits.
  • Severance exposure is described as shape only (full statutory aggregate salary — broader than base). Not a calculator.
  • Harassment / discrimination / bullying: Recognize → Report immediately to ER → Confidentiality → Zero retaliation. Do not investigate “off the record.”
  • Inaction on harassment can support employee rescission with cause attributable to the employer (full-severance-equivalent exposure).
  • NOM-035 / NOM-037: name-checked as mandatory protocols; managers support and escalate — no live teaching of questionnaires or remote-work reimbursements.

5. Handout 1 — Statutory Benefits Summary & Payroll Guide

Distributes end of Session 1

Disclaimer: Internal manager reference. Does not replace Mexican labor law, individual employment agreements, company policies, or case-specific HR/Payroll/Legal advice. Confirm amounts, cutoffs, and entity-specific rules with Payroll before acting.

1. Statutory benefits overview

Statutory benefits under the FLL are non-waivable and accrue from Day 1, including trial or training periods.

Statutory benefit Statutory minimum requirement Manager responsibility
Aguinaldo (Christmas Bonus) Mandatory year-end bonus of 30 days’ salary, paid by December each year (Payroll calculates per contract/entity). Plan workloads, approvals, and schedules early so December payroll is not disrupted.
Vacation & Vacation Premium Vacation: generally after 12 months; typically up to 18 months to use. Prima Vacacional: mandatory 25% premium on vacation pay. Approve leave fairly in Workday; encourage rest; do not pressure employees to forgo vacation.
PTU (Profit Sharing) Statutory profit sharing within the annual statutory window. No direct action. Route questions to HR/Payroll.
IMSS (Social Security) Mandatory enrollment for medical, risk, disability, and retirement coverage. Notify HR of confirmed start dates in time for Day 1 registration.
INFONAVIT (Housing Fund) Mandatory employer contributions; may include payroll deductions for housing credits. No direct action. Managed by HR and Payroll.
Sunday Premium (Prima Dominical) Additional 25% premium on daily salary when the regular schedule includes Sunday. Factor Sunday work into scheduling; confirm patterns with Payroll.

2. Manager vs. HR / Payroll ownership

Task / activity Manager owns HR / Payroll / Finance owns
Time & overtime Pre-authorize extraordinary hours; log/review actual hours in Workday same day or week. Calculate statutory premiums; verify weekly caps; process pay runs.
Leave & vacations Approve leave promptly; plan coverage. Accruals, vacation premium, legal usage windows.
New-hire onboarding Timely requisitions, offer details, confirmed start dates. IMSS/INFONAVIT registration; Day 1 contract execution.
Employee pay questions Listen and route immediately to HR/Payroll. Pay stubs, withholdings, resolution.

3. Critical overtime & payroll rules

Manager alert: Informal payroll arrangements are illegal. Never suggest, approve, or tolerate off-payroll, cash-in-hand, gift cards, or “we’ll true it up later.” Every peso must run through proper payroll with statutory contributions.
  1. No substitutions: Do not replace overtime with bonuses, flat allowances, informal comp time, or time off in lieu.
  2. Mandatory logging: Accurate Workday records; missing records weigh against the employer.
  3. Weekly caps: Max 12 extraordinary hours/week (4 hrs/day, max 4 days). Exceeding the cap requires Legal and Payroll review before implementation.
  4. Premiums: Payroll applies statutory premiums. Managers verify hours and approvals before cutoff — they do not calculate double/triple rates.

4. Contacts, payroll cutoffs & escalation

Payroll cutoffs: Dates change by entity and period. Before approving late hours or leave that affects the current cycle, confirm the active cutoff with Payroll (payroll@insulet.com). Do not assume prior-month dates.

1. Consequence matrix for non-compliance

Use to understand why escalation matters — not to quote a dollar amount. Exposure bands are directional only.

Manager action / misstep Legal & regulatory risk Financial & structural exposure Reputational
Misclassifying as contractor / improper outsourcing Reclassification; 2021 subcontracting sanctions High — back pay, benefits, retroactive IMSS/INFONAVIT, fines Medium
Unrecorded / unpaid OT or “bonus” substitutions STPS findings; labor claims Medium–High — back pay at statutory premiums + interest/fines Medium
Fixed-term lapse while work continues Auto-conversion to indefinite Medium — lost term protections; future severance liability Low
Terminating without documented legal cause Unjustified dismissal claim High — full statutory aggregate severance (base + bonuses, allowances, benefits in kind; shape only — Payroll/Legal calculate) + costs High
Ignoring / mishandling harassment or discrimination report Rescission with cause attributable to employer; authority action High — full statutory severance equivalent + fines Very High
Off-payroll / cash / informal compensation Tax, IMSS, INFONAVIT violations High — assessments, contributions, fines, personal exposure High
Omitting NOM-035 / mandatory protocols STPS inspection findings Low–Medium — administrative fines indexed to UMA Medium
Inconsistent discipline across the team Discrimination / pretext claims Medium–High — severance + potential damages High

Note: Fines and indemnities are often indexed to the UMA or minimum-wage multiples, which rise annually. Treat prior figures as stale.

2. Red flags — stop and escalate immediately

  • Contract expiration: Fixed-term end date within weeks, or passed while work continues.
  • Overtime thresholds: Unauthorized OT, or approaching the 12-hour weekly cap.
  • Informal deals: Cash, gift cards, allowances, bonuses, or informal comp time instead of Workday overtime.
  • Contemplating exit: Ending employment for any reason — ER/Legal and CFCRL; never DIY.
  • Safety & workplace reports: Harassment, discrimination, violence, bullying, or retaliation.
  • Differential treatment: Temptation to discipline one employee differently from a comparable colleague.
